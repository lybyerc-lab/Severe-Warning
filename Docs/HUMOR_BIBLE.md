# Severe Weather Warning Humor Bible

Status: canonical product-direction companion
Last updated: 2026-08-10

This document defines the recurring comedy backbone of **Severe Weather Warning** so the game's humor survives new levels, new workers, chat turnover, and future expansion.

## Core humor law

**Cows and Moo Brew are the comic backbone of the Severe Weather Warning universe.**

They are not isolated Easter eggs and they are not disposable one-note jokes. They are recurring identity anchors that can connect levels, newspapers, progression, unlocks, environmental storytelling, cinematics, scorecards, advertisements, statistics, and player-created chaos.

The storm fantasy must still be genuinely cool. The comedy works best when the world treats absurd bovine and Moo Brew circumstances with dead-serious civic, commercial, journalistic, or bureaucratic importance.

## The basic comic engine

A useful Severe Weather Warning joke often follows this contrast:

`serious storm spectacle or official presentation -> completely straight-faced bovine/Moo Brew detail -> escalation or callback`

Examples of the tone:

- a newspaper calmly reporting enormous cow-airtime statistics beside real destruction totals;
- a Moo Brew advertisement surviving in an otherwise devastated commercial block;
- an official warning that somehow contains a suspicious bovine clause;
- a fairground, stadium, pier, or civic event having an implausibly prominent Moo Brew sponsorship;
- Cow 17 behaving like a recurring local celebrity, employee, survivor, witness, or problem that officials are tired of explaining;
- unlock text treating a ridiculous cow-related feat as if it were a sanctioned athletic accomplishment.

The joke is usually strongest when nobody in-universe admits that it is a joke.

## Cows

Cows are recurring comic characters, statistics, environmental beats, unlock hooks, and sources of emergent spectacle.

They may appear as:

- physical cows in appropriate locations;
- Cow 17 appearances;
- newspaper photographs, headlines, sidebars, or statistics;
- legal notices and municipal warnings;
- unlock conditions and secret-level folklore;
- MOO-LAH iconography;
- signage, mascots, trophies, records, or sponsor material;
- recurring references that imply the county has a much larger bovine problem than anyone wants to discuss.

### Cow safety law

Cows are not ordinary disposable destruction targets.

The humor comes from relocation, airtime, improbable survival, official concern, escape, statistics, and recurring identity. Do not turn the central cow joke into animal-harm comedy.

Cow 17 remains especially protected as a recurring personality/mascot unless an explicit later design contract changes that role.

## Cow 17

Cow 17 is a recurring character anchor, not merely a single cinematic actor.

Useful future roles may include:

- opening/ending visual callbacks;
- newspaper witness quotes or photographs;
- Moo Brew employee/sponsor imagery;
- secret-level appearances;
- ridiculous records or statistics;
- background cameos;
- unlock celebrations;
- occasional silent reaction shots;
- a recurring implication that Cow 17 has somehow been present for far too many severe-weather incidents.

Do not require Cow 17 to appear in every level. Scarcity makes cameos more valuable.

## Cow-fucius incubator

**Cow-fucius** is an approved humor-incubator character/name inspired by the Confucius wordplay.

Do not force Cow-fucius into the main story or assign a mandatory gameplay role yet. Preserve the name and explore it as a recurring background/callback opportunity whose value comes from appearing unexpectedly and being treated with complete seriousness.

### Preferred first role: loading-screen philosopher

The strongest current fit for Cow-fucius is as a recurring **loading-screen / transition philosopher**. This gives the character repeated exposure without interrupting gameplay, and allows his sayings to become a familiar part of the game's rhythm.

Cow-fucius loading screens may combine:

- a portrait, woodcut, newspaper engraving, statue-like pose, or other stylized likeness;
- one short saying that is either genuinely useful, almost useful, or absurdly philosophical;
- occasional real gameplay guidance disguised as Cow-fucius wisdom;
- occasional references to score chasing, cooldown discipline, route choice, MOO-LAH, cows, destruction, or weather;
- rare callbacks that change after major unlocks or discoveries;
- a small Moo Brew sponsorship mark or endorsement when appropriate.

Loading-screen copy should remain brief enough that it can be read before the transition finishes. Do not make loading screens linger merely to deliver a joke.

### Moo Brew relationship: intentionally unresolved

Cow-fucius may eventually become a **Moo Brew spokesperson, official philosopher, licensed likeness, historical mascot, or suspicious corporate endorsement figure**, but that status is deliberately not locked yet.

Preferred early treatment: Moo Brew behaves as though it has a relationship with Cow-fucius, while the exact nature of that relationship remains unclear. Examples might include a tiny `Presented by Moo Brew` stamp, a dubious quotation credit, or a corporate footer beneath one of his sayings.

This ambiguity is part of the joke. Do not immediately reduce Cow-fucius to an ordinary advertising mascot unless later playtest/writing work proves that version stronger.

Possible uses include:

- tiny newspaper quote boxes or "wisdom of the day" sidebars;
- loading or transition aphorisms;
- Moo Brew fortune-cookie-style promotional copy;
- a statue, portrait, mural, shrine, library book, museum exhibit, or civic plaque;
- rare Weather Map sayings;
- absurdly profound commentary on destruction, cows, MOO-LAH, weather, or player behavior;
- a secret collectible set of Cow-fucius sayings;
- a Cow Level cameo or unlock flavor if it earns the joke.

Tone rule: the sayings should sound *almost* wise, then bend toward the Severe Weather Warning universe. Avoid relying on nonstop "moo" substitutions. The joke should survive because Cow-fucius appears to have a coherent bovine philosophy, not because every sentence is a pun.

Potential example tone, for later writing only:

- "Cow-fucius says: fence that stands before storm is merely future lumber."
- "Cow-fucius says: he who chases high score should first respect low barn."
- "Cow-fucius says: MOO-LAH saved is MOO-LAH not yet irresponsibly spent."
- "Cow-fucius says: wise storm watches cooldown; wiser storm has already chosen next target."

These examples establish direction, not final shipping copy.

## Moo Brew

**Moo Brew is the recurring commercial/comedy brand of the universe.**

It can function as the connective corporate tissue that makes unrelated destruction sites feel like they exist in the same world.

Possible appearances include:

- cups, cans, vending machines, coolers, delivery trucks, billboards, signs, and storefronts;
- newspaper display ads, coupons, classifieds, sponsor boxes, or legal disclaimers;
- stadium/fairground/amusement-park sponsorship;
- concession stands at piers, zoos, malls, schools/events, parks, and other appropriate public places;
- scorecard sponsorship jokes;
- branded destruction targets where appropriate;
- collectible or unlock flavor;
- recurring slogans that evolve after disasters;
- fake corporate safety messaging;
- suspiciously extensive local-market dominance.

Moo Brew should feel like a brand that really exists inside this world, not a logo pasted onto every surface.

## Humor across the game loop

### Opening / pre-run

Newspaper presentation, storm selection, and **UNLEASH STORM** can use Cow 17, Moo Brew, bovine warnings, sponsor copy, and absurd local headlines as flavor while keeping storm choice and launch actions immediately readable.

### During a run

Comedy should mostly emerge from the world and the player's actions rather than constant text jokes.

Good sources include:

- unexpected cow relocation;
- Moo Brew signage/props reacting to destruction;
- environmental callbacks;
- rare Cow 17 cameos;
- ridiculous but readable challenge notifications;
- physical situations the player creates.

Do not bury gameplay in jokes or popups.

### End-of-run newspaper

This is one of the richest comedy surfaces.

The results edition can report serious destruction beside absurd recurring details such as:

- cows relocated;
- combined bovine airtime;
- Cow 17 status/cameo;
- Moo Brew property damage or sponsorship notes;
- MOO-LAH earned;
- new bovine records;
- weird legal disclaimers;
- local official quotes;
- callbacks to secrets or unusual feats.

The page should still communicate score, rank, progression, rewards, and replay actions first.

## Expansion-site law

New Storm Sites do **not** need cows physically present in every map.

The humor backbone may travel through other channels:

- Moo Brew sponsorship at an amusement park;
- a bovine classified in a train-station newspaper box;
- Cow 17 on a zoo billboard;
- a stadium Moo Brew concession contract;
- a library poster about an inexplicably overdue bovine-related book;
- a pier advertisement for Moo Brew next to a Waterspout feat;
- a mall kiosk, delivery truck, scorecard headline, or unlock reference.

This preserves the shared universe without making every location visually repetitive.

## Comedy escalation and callbacks

Prefer recurring jokes that evolve.

A good recurring gag may begin as background flavor, later become an objective or unlock, then reappear in a newspaper headline or another location.

Examples:

- Moo Brew starts as a cup -> becomes a storefront -> becomes a sponsor -> becomes a ridiculous corporate incident report;
- cows begin as harmless farm life -> become tracked airtime statistics -> unlock the Moo Level -> become part of competitive bovine records;
- Cow 17 begins as a cinematic character -> becomes a recurring local-news figure whose survival history grows increasingly implausible.

Callbacks should reward returning players without confusing new ones.

## What to avoid

Do not let the humor become:

- nonstop cow puns;
- a cow decal on every object;
- random meme references that date the game quickly;
- dialogue-heavy joke delivery that interrupts play;
- cruelty toward animals;
- humor that makes the storm itself feel weak or silly all the time;
- Moo Brew branding so dense that locations lose their own identity;
- jokes that obscure objectives, controls, rewards, or progression.

## Tone target

The world of Severe Weather Warning should feel capable of delivering a genuinely spectacular tornado sequence and then, without changing its facial expression, report that **31 cows achieved unauthorized flight** and that **Moo Brew reminds residents its cups are not rated for EF-scale conditions**.

That tension is the identity.
