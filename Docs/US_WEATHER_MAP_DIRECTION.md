# Severe Weather Warning U.S. Weather Map Direction

Status: canonical product-direction companion
Last updated: 2026-08-10

## Core idea

The primary destination-selection layer should evoke the memorable 1990s game pattern where the player sees a map of the United States and chooses an unlocked place to play.

For Severe Weather Warning, this becomes a stylized national **Weather Map** that acts as both progression screen and fantasy map.

The current town/county campaign remains the home territory and early backbone of the game. It is not replaced by the national map. Instead, the national map grows outward from those first counties into a larger library of destruction destinations.

## Desired player experience

The map should make the player want to know what is hiding elsewhere before they even press **UNLEASH STORM**.

A player should be able to see:

- unlocked towns/counties;
- larger regional destinations;
- focused destruction sites;
- secret levels;
- bonus score-attack sites;
- locked locations that tease how they might be opened;
- unusual coastal, civic, retail, amusement, industrial, farm, stadium, school, zoo, beach, pier, rail, airport, junkyard, and event-ground destinations;
- visual hints for special storm-form unlocks or feats.

The map should feel like a growing road trip of severe-weather opportunities rather than a linear level list.

## 1990s-map spirit

Use the clarity and anticipation of older location-select games:

- readable U.S. silhouette;
- obvious regional markers;
- locked/unlocked state visible at a glance;
- satisfying reveal when a new destination opens;
- destination labels with strong personality;
- simple navigation first, decorative detail second;
- a sense that more of the country is waiting beyond the current route.

Do not copy a specific legacy game's UI. Capture the broad interaction fantasy and readability.

## Scale law

The map may contain different play-space scales.

### Large destinations

Examples: counties, towns, amusement/beach districts, larger boardwalk areas, regional complexes.

These may support multiple routes, landmarks, secrets, and longer runs.

### Focused destinations

Examples: stadium, fairground, mall, zoo, airport, school campus, rail yard, civic district.

These remain substantial play spaces but are more concentrated.

### Tighter destruction playgrounds

Examples: junkyard, drive-in theater, train station, grocery complex, park, small event grounds.

These may be shorter and denser, but must never feel like a tiny arena or disposable minigame box. The tornado still needs room to move, build momentum, and create chains of destruction.

## Unlock law

Locations can unlock through multiple systems:

- campaign progress;
- score/rank thresholds;
- MOO-LAH purchases where appropriate;
- secrets;
- special destruction feats;
- storm-form discoveries;
- bonus-level completion;
- unusual world interactions.

Not every node should explain its unlock condition immediately. Some secret destinations should encourage rumor, experimentation, and player-to-player discovery.

Examples already established:

- complete the Hart Farm Bovine Compliance Test -> unlock MOO LEVEL;
- launch a boat on a coastal site -> unlock Waterspout storm form/skin.

## Presentation identity

The map should still belong to the Severe Weather Warning universe.

Possible presentation language includes:

- forecast-map styling;
- weather-service markings;
- county/state boundaries;
- storm-track lines;
- newspaper callouts and local headlines;
- Moo Brew sponsor markers used sparingly;
- bovine/weather bureaucracy jokes where they help rather than clutter.

The map itself should be attractive enough to become part of the game's identity, not a generic node screen.

## Design priority

The map exists to create anticipation, progression clarity, and a sense of a larger world.

It should support the broader law:

**Severe Weather Warning is a growing collection of places players want to unleash a tornado, anchored by the original town/county campaign and expanded across the United States.**
