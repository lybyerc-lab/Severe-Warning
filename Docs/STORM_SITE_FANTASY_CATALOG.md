# Severe Weather Warning Storm Site Fantasy Catalog

Status: canonical expansion inventory
Last updated: 2026-08-10

This catalog captures the central content fantasy for Severe Weather Warning:

> **Places you always wanted to unleash a tornado.**

The Storm Site system should be able to keep growing for a long time. Not every location needs a campaign excuse, a unique narrative, or the same objective template. Some sites can simply be exceptionally satisfying destruction playgrounds.

The creative test for a site is simple:

> Would somebody grin if you said, "You can release a tornado in that?"

## Amusement park / theme park

Core fantasy: **release a tornado inside a RollerCoaster-Tycoon-style park and let the systems collide.**

Useful anatomy:
- roller coasters and track supports;
- ferris wheel;
- drop tower;
- carousel;
- swinging/pirate-ship rides;
- bumper cars;
- midway stalls;
- food courts/stands;
- ticket gates;
- maintenance sheds;
- themed facades;
- benches, trash cans, signs, fencing, queue rails, lamps;
- parking areas and shuttle/tram props.

High-value destruction moments:
- progressive coaster-support failure;
- cars/ride pieces becoming dramatic debris without requiring visible guest harm;
- chain reactions across tightly packed rides;
- sign towers collapsing into midway structures;
- landmark ride as a score/objective centerpiece.

This family can support multiple parks with different themes rather than one single amusement-park level.

## Zoo / animal park

Core fantasy: a large landscaped public attraction full of themed structures, signs, service roads, exhibits, water features, and unusual architecture.

Animal-safety law still applies. The destruction game should target structures and environment, not turn animals into disposable victims.

Useful anatomy:
- entrance arch;
- exhibit buildings;
- aviaries;
- fences/barriers;
- food courts;
- gift shop;
- tram stops;
- maintenance/service areas;
- decorative rockwork;
- water features;
- playground/kids zone;
- zoo signage and maps.

Possible comedy can come from escaped signage, absurd newspaper reporting, or animals safely reacting/relocating rather than damage.

## Train station / rail yard

Useful anatomy:
- station building;
- platforms and shelters;
- pedestrian overpass;
- clocks/signage;
- rail yard structures;
- parked rail cars;
- maintenance sheds;
- signal gantries;
- power/utility structures;
- parking/drop-off areas.

High-value feats:
- launch or relocate a rail car;
- chain destruction through signal/power infrastructure;
- collapse a station canopy in a readable staged way.

## Library / civic campus

Useful anatomy:
- civic architecture;
- columns/awnings;
- book-return structures;
- public art;
- parking;
- landscaping;
- nearby courthouse/city-hall/plaza modules where appropriate.

Tone should keep the comedy in local-news framing and absurd civic seriousness rather than cruelty.

## Downtown / city variants

Do not chase density at the expense of mobile performance or readable destruction anatomy.

## Site-content philosophy

A location does not need to justify itself through narrative if its destruction fantasy is strong enough.

Good examples:
- "Destroy the fairground."
- "Release the tornado in the amusement park."
- "Run the beach."
- "Tear through the strip mall."

Objectives, secrets, and progression can layer on top without becoming prerequisites for fun.

## Content scalability law

The library should be designed to keep expanding without exhausting the premise.

New Storm Sites should reuse common systemic pieces where smart, but each site should have at least one or two **signature destruction moments** that make it worth remembering and replaying.

The game does not need a finite list of four, eight, or twelve locations as its identity. The long-term fantasy is a growing map full of places where the player thinks:

> "I want to see what the tornado does to that."
